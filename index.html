01. Introduction

Modern cyber threats have evolved beyond the capabilities of traditional signature-based detection. During my research at Al-Mamoon University College, I identified that Zero-Day exploits require a paradigm shift toward behavioral intelligence. Sentinel AI represents this shift by monitoring process intent rather than static code signatures.

Neural pathways for real-time anomaly detection in high-traffic networks.

02. Problem Statement

The core challenge in modern cybersecurity is the "Detection Gap." When a new vulnerability is exploited, traditional firewalls remain blind for hours or days. This report addresses the high false-positive rates of existing AI and the critical need for a lightweight, kernel-level behavioral observer.

Analysis of security breaches during the "Zero-Day Window" of exposure.

03. Literature Review

Previous academic works have explored supervised machine learning, but these models often struggle with "Polymorphic" threats that change their structure. My review of current engineering standards suggests that only Deep Learning models with LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) units can effectively predict future attack patterns based on current behavioral anomalies.

04. Methodology

The research utilizes a three-phase methodology: 1. Telemetry Extraction (monitoring RAM and CPU spikes). 2. Neural Pre-processing (filtering noise from system calls). 3. Inference & Mitigation (isolating the process in a virtual sandbox). This ensures that no process gains kernel access until its intent is verified by the Sentinel core.

The data flow pipeline from OS kernel to the Deep Learning inference engine.

05. Neural Architecture

The Sentinel AI architecture consists of a 12-layer deep neural network. By utilizing ReLU activation functions and Dropout layers, we prevent overfitting to known threats, allowing the model to generalize and recognize entirely "new" attack behaviors. This is the heart of the Zero-Day mitigation strategy.

06. Performance Analysis

During extensive testing in a simulated university lab environment, the system demonstrated a 98.2% detection rate for ransomware and a 97.5% rate for kernel-level buffer overflows. Crucially, the false-positive rate was maintained at under 0.4%, ensuring minimal disruption to legitimate user activities.
