1. Introduction

Background of the Study

Historically, cybersecurity was a game of "Cat and Mouse" where defenses always lagged behind the attackers. In my analysis of the 2026 threat landscape, it is evident that static firewalls are no longer enough. Hackers now use AI to generate "Zero-Day" exploits that change their appearance every second. This research introduces Sentinel AI, a proactive shield designed at Al-Mamoon University College to catch these invisible threats.

Panel A Analysis: Most modern breaches exploit the "Detection Gap" where traditional antivirus tools fail to recognize new malware patterns (Source 1).
Figure 1: Comparison of traditional vs. AI-driven attack vectors.

The significance of this study lies in its shift towards "Behavioral Intelligence." Instead of looking at what a file *is*, my framework analyzes what the file *does* once it enters the memory. This ensures that even the most complex, encrypted ransomware can be identified by its actions.

2. Problem Statement

The core challenge in cybersecurity engineering today is the "Signal-to-Noise" ratio. Network administrators are overwhelmed by thousands of false alerts daily. This "Alert Fatigue" allows real Zero-Day attacks to slip through unnoticed. In this report, I define the problem as the inability of current systems to distinguish between legitimate technical tools and malicious exploitation attempts in real-time.

Panel B Objective: The primary goal is to achieve a detection accuracy of 97.8% while maintaining a processing latency of less than 200ms per packet.
Figure 2: Conceptual mapping of the Research Objectives and KPI metrics.

By solving this, we protect critical infrastructure—from banking to government sectors—ensuring that the digital backbone of Iraq remains secure. Sentinel AI is not just a theoretical model; it is a necessary evolution in our defense strategy against increasingly intelligent adversaries.
