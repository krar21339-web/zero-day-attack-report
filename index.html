03. Executive Introduction

In my journey as a student at Al-Mamoon University College, I've seen how the digital landscape is shifting. We are no longer fighting simple viruses; we are fighting autonomous code. Traditional firewalls act like old locks—they work until someone finds a new way to pick them.

Figure 3.1: Analysis of unknown threat vectors in modern network infrastructures.

"Sentinel AI" is my attempt to build a smarter lock. By using Deep Learning, we aren't just looking for bad files; we are looking for bad behavior. If a program starts acting like a thief, the system stops it, even if it has never seen that program before. This is the future of the Iraqi digital defense.

04. Problem Statement

The core problem we face today is the "Window of Vulnerability." When a new "Zero-Day" attack is launched, there is a period of time where no one knows how to stop it. During this time, hospitals, banks, and universities are completely exposed.

Through my research, I identified that the primary gap is the reliance on signatures. Most systems are "reactive." They wait for the attack to happen, then they learn. My proposal is to make them "proactive." We need models that can predict a threat based on the way it interacts with the CPU and Memory.

The challenges I am addressing include reducing false alarms (False Positives) and ensuring the AI can run on a normal laptop without slowing it down.
