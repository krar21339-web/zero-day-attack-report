03. Executive Introduction

In the modern landscape of cyber warfare, the velocity of attack execution has fundamentally bypassed human reaction time. During my studies at Al-Mamoon University College, I observed that traditional security perimeters are failing. The reason is simple: they look for "known" signatures. But what happens when the threat has never been seen before? This is the realm of the Zero-Day exploit.

Figure 3.1 Analysis: Visualization of neural network flow detecting anomalies in real-time traffic.

Sentinel AI is my proposed solution. It doesn't look for a "name" or a "fingerprint." Instead, it analyzes behavior. By leveraging Deep Learning, we can train a system to understand the "soul" of a normal process, making any deviation instantly visible, regardless of how new the virus is.

04. Problem Statement

The core challenge in contemporary cybersecurity is the "Detection Gap." Current firewalls and EDR systems are reactive by design. They wait for a disaster to happen, record the signature, and then protect others. This leaves the "Zero-Day" victim completely vulnerable.

In my analysis, I identified three primary gaps in current AI models used for security: 1. Computational Overload: Most deep learning models are too heavy for real-time monitoring on standard laptops. 2. False Positives: Distinguishing between a heavy software update and a malicious data exfiltration is still a major hurdle. 3. Metamorphic Resilience: Modern viruses can change their own code to bypass static detection.

My research aims to close this window by implementing a lightweight LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) network that operates at the kernel level without causing system lag.
