Section 04

Threat Analysis

In the ecosystem of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), the ESP32 serves as a critical node. Our deep-dive analysis reveals that traffic emulation isn't just about data; it's about identifying the Attack Vectors that could compromise national infrastructure.

[Image: Cyber Attack Map or Global Threat Visualization]
01. Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) & Flooding

By emulating high-velocity UDP/TCP traffic, we simulate a stress test on the network gateway. An attacker could exploit the ESP32's limited buffer to cause a Kernel Panic or memory exhaustion.
Technical Impact: Complete service blackout and resource starvation (RAM/CPU).

02. Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) & Packet Sniffing

During our injection phase, we analyzed the 802.11 Frame Structure. Without WPA3 or TLS 1.3, the traffic payload is transparent to packet sniffers (like Wireshark). This allows for credential harvesting and session hijacking.

03. De-authentication & Firmware Reverse Engineering

Attackers can send De-auth Frames to disconnect IoT nodes. Furthermore, if the JTAG port isn't disabled, the firmware can be dumped and reverse-engineered to find hardcoded API keys.

Final Security Recommendation:

Implementing Secure Boot and Flash Encryption on the ESP32 is the only way to ensure the integrity of the IoT traffic emulation environment.
