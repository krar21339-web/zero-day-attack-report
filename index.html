1. Introduction

Background of the Study

The digital infrastructure of 2026 is under a state of constant siege. As I have documented in this research, the traditional boundaries of cybersecurity have been dissolved by the advent of AI-driven malware. Historically, we relied on "Known Signatures"—a reactive method that only stops threats we have seen before. However, my analysis shows that modern attackers now use polymorphic code to change their digital fingerprint every few seconds, making static defense tools obsolete.

2. Problem Statement

The primary challenge I am addressing in this report is the "Invisibility of Sophisticated Malware." Today's threats are not just files; they are living processes that hide inside legitimate applications. This creates a "Signal-to-Noise" problem for security engineers at Al-Mamoon University and beyond. When a system generates thousands of alerts, the real threat is often missed.

Significance & Objectives

This study is significant because it provides a roadmap for "Proactive Defense." My objectives are threefold: First, to decrease detection latency to under 200ms. Second, to achieve a 97% accuracy rate against previously unseen exploits. Third, to ensure the system is lightweight enough to run on standard hardware without slowing down the user.
